# Community biolab websites: research notes

How peer community biolabs present themselves, and what Biopunk should borrow.
Compiled from Genspace, BioCurious, and BUGSS.

## The shared spine
Across the strong community-lab sites, the same structure repeats:

1. **Biosafety stated up front.** They name their level (all BSL-1) and link to a real
   handbook or the CDC guidance. Safety is framed as credibility, not fine print.
2. **Tiered membership by who the member is**, not just by price:
   - Genspace: Community (collective learning) / Individual (own research) / Premium (business, adds dedicated storage).
   - BUGSS: Community (trades volunteer hours for access) / Power (pure lab access) / Entrepreneur (bundled with an incubator).
   - BioCurious: a flat monthly rate (historically ~$100-150) plus classes.
3. **A human join flow.** Application is a form, then a real conversation: rolling review,
   a lab-manager reach-out, and a tour/orientation before anyone gets bench access.
   Genspace routes beginners into a Biohacker Boot Camp class first.
4. **"Community projects" as the engagement engine.** Named, ongoing projects (3D bioprinting,
   Real Vegan Cheese, OpenPlant) are what pull people in and give members something to join.
5. **A consistent nav:** About / Membership / Classes / Events / Projects / FAQ / Get Involved.

## Per-lab specifics
- **Genspace** (Brooklyn): BSL-1 up front, three tiers, applications via an Airtable form,
  rolling review, lab-manager meeting + walkthrough. Publishes a community-bio biosafety handbook.
  Positions as "the world's first community biology lab."
- **BioCurious** (Santa Clara): flat monthly membership plus classes; equipment list front and center;
  volunteer-run. Positions as "the world's largest community lab space for biology."
- **BUGSS** (Baltimore): most transparent on safety. States plainly that community labs stay safe by
  vetting and approving member projects, posting safety procedures, and following the DIY-bio code of
  ethics. New materials go through an approval step against an inventory. Month-to-month, orientation
  meeting before access, Entrepreneur tier bundled with the Harbor Launch incubator.

## What Biopunk should borrow (all now built into the site)
- **Name tiers by member type**: Community / Resident / Startup-Team.
- **Show the join flow as a transparent sequence** so people know a human and a safety step stand
  between them and a bench (built into membership.html and the homepage).
- **Make safety visible** as a trust signal, not hidden (the safety section).
- **A bookable-equipment list** so the "what do I actually get" question is answered on-page.

## Positioning note
Biopunk's hero claims "world's largest open-access biolab community." BioCurious already brands as
"world's largest community lab space," and Genspace as "world's first." The superlative is contested.
**"Open-access" is the harder-to-challenge differentiator**, so lean there.
